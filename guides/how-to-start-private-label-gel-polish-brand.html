Quick Answer

What buyers need to knowBegin with one defined customer, a small coordinated color range, a proven formula route and standard packaging. Test samples as a complete system, confirm the market document plan, calculate landed cost, then approve written specifications and artwork.

The eight-step launch checklist

Build the commercial and technical brief before buying inventory.

1. Define the customer

Choose salons, technicians, consumers or distributors; needs and price points differ.

2. Set the market

Name each country to guide ingredient, label, document and logistics planning.

3. Design the system

Specify base, color and top together and start with a focused shade range.

4. Select the route

Compare standard, adjusted and custom formulas by sampling, MOQ and cost.

5. Choose packaging

Confirm bottle, cap, brush, label or print, box, barcode and carton.

6. Test samples

Record application, curing, finish, wear, removal and package observations.

7. Calculate landed cost

Add decoration, testing, freight, duties, storage, returns and promotion.

8. Approve specifications

Connect product codes, samples, artwork versions and packing instructions.

Common first-launch mistakes

A focused, documented project is easier to test and reorder.

Too many shades

Every shade adds sample review, inventory and forecasting.

Claims before evidence

Build label language from the confirmed formula and market review.

Packaging in isolation

Test formula, brush, bottle and curing system together.

Unit-price budgeting

Use landed cost to protect the real sales margin.

Vague approvals

Approve named formula codes, shades, artwork and packaging.

No reorder records

Preserve product codes, samples and files for batch comparison.

Buyer questions

Is MOQ total or per color?

Ask for a written breakdown by SKU. Tiny quantities across many shades may not be practical.

Which sample parts match production?

Confirm formula, color, brush, bottle, decoration and box, noting any temporary components.

Which documents match my formula?

Match ingredient lists, SDS or tests to the exact product code being purchased.

What can be excluded from a quote?

Check artwork setup, printing, boxes, testing, cartons, freight, duties and payment fees.
